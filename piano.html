Sound familiar?

You don't need more theory. You need to hear it.

Maybe you took lessons as a kid and can still only play from the page. Maybe you watch players at church or online make it look effortless and wonder what they know that you don't. They aren't reading. They are hearing patterns: the same handful of chords and progressions, moved around in every key. That's a skill, and it can be taught.

The number system

Think in 1, 4, 5 and 6 instead of letter names, so a song you learn in one key works in all twelve.

Find the key, fast

A simple routine to land on the key of any song in under a minute, from your phone, the radio or a worship set.

Progressions you'll hear everywhere

1-4-5, 1-5-6-4, 2-5-1 and the rest. Learn them as sounds and you'll start hearing them in every song.

Pick out melodies

Train your ear to find a tune note by note, then put the right chord underneath it.

Voicings that sound pro

Sevenths, ninths, passing chords and the gospel and R&B colours that make simple songs sound rich.

Play with other people

Rhythm, grooves and following a band or worship leader, so you're ready the day someone says "you're on keys".

Questions

Piano FAQ

Do I need to read music?

No. We teach by ear and by pattern. If you already read, great: you'll learn to step away from the page. If you don't, you never have to start.

What kind of keyboard do I need at home?

Any keyboard with at least 61 keys to start. A digital piano with 88 weighted keys is ideal if you're buying. Ask us before you buy and we'll point you to good options at every budget.

Is this only for gospel or worship music?

Not at all. The same skills work for pop, R&B, rock, jazz standards and film themes. Church musicians love it because worship sets are played by ear, but you bring the songs.

How fast will I be able to play a song?

Most beginners play a simple song with both hands within their first few weeks. Playing anything by ear takes longer, but you'll hear yourself getting better every month.
